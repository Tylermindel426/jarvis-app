Privacy policy for Jarvis

Last updated: November 14, 2026

Jarvis is a personal assistant that only its owner uses, on the owner's own computer. This page explains what it does with Google and YouTube data.

What Jarvis accesses

Jarvis gets access to a YouTube account only when its owner grants it on Google's sign-in screen. It asks for two permissions:

  • View your YouTube account. Used to read the channel's name and ID, and the status of its videos.
  • Manage your YouTube videos. Used to upload and schedule videos on that channel.

Jarvis uses this access only to post the owner's own videos on the owner's own channel, and to confirm they were published. If Jarvis ever needs more, Google's sign-in screen lists it before anything is granted.

What Jarvis stores

The permission from Google (an access token) is stored only on the owner's computer, outside any shared or backed-up folder. Jarvis also keeps a local record of each upload: the video ID, title and schedule. Jarvis has no server, so nothing is stored anywhere else.

What Jarvis shares

Nothing. Jarvis does not sell, share or transfer data to anyone. Data only moves between the owner's computer and Google/YouTube.

YouTube API Services

Jarvis uses the YouTube API Services. See the YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy.

Removing access

Access can be removed at any time on the Google Account permissions page: myaccount.google.com/permissions. Deleting the token file on the owner's computer also ends Jarvis's access. Videos that were already uploaded stay on YouTube until they are removed in YouTube Studio.

Contact

The developer's contact email is shown on Google's sign-in screen for Jarvis.
